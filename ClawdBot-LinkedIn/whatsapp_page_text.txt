Why your outbound | RevyOps | Alan C.
Your outbound agency can generate meetings and still struggle to prove ROI.

Most reports focus on activity:
open rates, reply rates, meetings booked, emails sent.

Those numbers show execution, not impact.

The real questions are different:
Which campaign created pipeline?
Which segment converts into revenue?
Which sequence led to closed deals?

If you can't answer these, the issue is attribution.

Leads enter the system with enrichment, but that data is not carried into the CRM in a structured way. Meetings get booked without linking back to campaigns or sequences. Deals move forward without retaining source data. By the time revenue is recorded, outbound is no longer connected.

This breaks visibility.

The fix is operational, not creative.

Each contact must enter the CRM with source and enrichment data.
Each meeting must be tied to campaign, sequence, and segment.
Each opportunity must retain that data through to closed-won.

When this flow is in place, reporting becomes clear.

You can see what drives pipeline, what converts, and where to invest.

Without it, outbound looks like activity.

With it, outbound becomes measurable.

I’ve been thinking about this a lot lately.

Coding used to be the moat.

If you could build, you had an edge.

Now with tools like Codex, Claude code, Cursor…
building is becoming easier every day.

You can go from idea → working prototype faster than ever.

You don’t need to be a “hardcore engineer” to ship something useful anymore.

Which sounds like a huge advantage…

until you realize something important:

If everyone can build, building is no longer the differentiator.

Distribution is.

It doesn’t matter how good your product is if no one sees it.
It doesn’t matter how clean your code is if no one uses it.
It doesn’t matter how smart you are if no one knows what you’re doing.

Whether you’re:

a founder trying to get users
someone looking for a job
or just building cool things on the side

The game has shifted.

It’s no longer just:

“Can you build?”

It’s:

“Can you get attention, trust, and users?”

And that’s where most people struggle.

People spent years learning how to code.

Very few of us learned how to distribute what we build.

Personally, this is the area I’m trying to get better at.

Because in a world where building is easier,
distribution becomes the real leverage.

I heard this quote from Naval Ravikant "If you know how to code and know how to market it, you become irreplaceable"

Curious, what are you focusing more on right now?

Building or distribution or Both?
