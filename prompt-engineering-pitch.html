System Message

The Invisible Director

The system message sets the AI's personality, constraints, and expertise before the conversation even starts. Your prospect never sees it, but it shapes every word of the output. Think of it as the job description you hand the AI.

// System message example "role": "system", "content": "You are a senior B2B sales consultant specializing in SaaS. You write concise, value-first outreach emails. Never use filler phrases like 'I hope this finds you well.' Always lead with a specific pain point relevant to the prospect's industry."
User Message

The Specific Ask

This is your input — the question, request, or data you feed the AI. The more specific and structured your user message, the better the output. Include context: who the prospect is, what you need, and any constraints.

// User message example "role": "user", "content": "Write a cold outreach email to a VP of Engineering at a mid-market fintech company. They recently raised a Series B. Our product reduces CI/CD pipeline failures by 40%. Keep it under 120 words."
Assistant Message

The Trained Response

The assistant message is the AI's reply — but it's also a teaching tool. By providing example assistant responses (called few-shot prompting), you train the AI on your exact tone, format, and quality bar. This is how you go from "pretty good" to "sounds like our top closer wrote it."

// Assistant message (few-shot example) "role": "assistant", "content": "Subject: 40% fewer pipeline failures post-Series B Hey {{first_name}}, Congrats on the Series B — scaling eng teams fast usually means CI/CD breaks increase 3x. We helped [Similar Company] cut pipeline failures by 40% within 60 days of deployment. Worth a 15-min call this week to see if the numbers work for {{company}}? Best, {{sender}}"
Part Two

How to Evaluate a Prompt

A prompt isn't "good" because it sounds smart. It's good when it reliably produces output that drives your desired outcome. Here's the framework.

◎

Relevance

Does the output directly address the scenario you described?

  • Output matches the persona you targeted
  • Industry-specific language is present
  • No off-topic tangents or generic filler
✓

Accuracy

Are the facts, numbers, and claims correct and verifiable?

  • Statistics are sourced or flagged as placeholders
  • Product claims match actual capabilities
  • No hallucinated company names or case studies
♬

Tone & Voice

Does it sound like your brand — not a robot, not a stranger?

  • Matches your team's communication style
  • Appropriate formality for the audience
  • No clichés or overused AI phrases
⚡

Actionability

Can your rep copy-paste and send, or does it need heavy editing?

  • Clear CTA or next step is included
  • Personalization tokens are usable
  • Length is appropriate for the channel

The Quick-Score Method

Run every prompt output through this 10-second check

Rate the output 1-5 on each dimension: Relevance ___/5 Is it on-target for this prospect? Accuracy ___/5 Are the claims defensible? Tone ___/5 Does it sound like us? Actionability ___/5 Can a rep send it right now? Total: ___/20 16-20 = Ship it | 11-15 = Tweak the prompt | 0-10 = Rewrite

3 Ready-to-Use Sales Prompts

Copy these into your AI tool of choice. Each prompt uses the system/user/assistant pattern you just learned. Customize the bracketed fields for your product and prospects.

Prompt 1

Cold Outreach Email Generator

Use Case: First-touch emails to net-new prospects

System
You are an elite B2B sales copywriter. You write cold outreach emails that are concise (under 100 words), lead with a specific pain point, and end with a soft CTA. Never use generic openers. Personalize based on the prospect's role, industry, and any recent company events provided.
User
Write a cold email to [PROSPECT NAME], [TITLE] at [COMPANY]. Industry: [INDUSTRY]. Recent news: [TRIGGER EVENT — e.g., new funding round, expansion, product launch]. Our product: [ONE-SENTENCE VALUE PROP]. Key metric: [STAT — e.g., "saves 12 hrs/week for ops teams"]. Tone: Professional but conversational. No buzzwords.
Example Output
Subject: [TRIGGER EVENT] + [KEY METRIC] Hi [FIRST NAME], Saw [COMPANY] just [TRIGGER EVENT] — exciting move. Teams scaling that fast usually hit [PAIN POINT]. We helped [SIMILAR COMPANY] [KEY METRIC] within [TIMEFRAME]. Open to a quick 15-min call this week to see if the numbers make sense for [COMPANY]? [SENDER]
Why This Works

The system message bans generic openers, so every output leads with relevance. The user message includes a trigger event, giving the AI real context to personalize. The few-shot example sets the exact structure and length you want.

Prompt 2

Discovery Call Prep Brief

Use Case: Pre-call research and question generation

System
You are a sales strategist who prepares discovery call briefs. For each prospect, produce: 1. A 3-sentence company summary (what they do, size, recent momentum) 2. Three potential pain points based on their industry and role 3. Five discovery questions — open-ended, insight-driven, not yes/no 4. One "power question" that reframes their thinking Use bullet points. Be direct. No fluff.
User
Prepare a discovery call brief for my meeting with: Prospect: [NAME], [TITLE] Company: [COMPANY] — [BRIEF DESCRIPTION] Industry: [INDUSTRY] Company size: [EMPLOYEES / REVENUE RANGE] What we sell: [YOUR PRODUCT/SERVICE] Known challenges in this space: [OPTIONAL CONTEXT]
Example Output
COMPANY SNAPSHOT [Company] is a [size] [industry] company that [core business]. They recently [momentum event]. Reports to [likely reporting structure]. LIKELY PAIN POINTS • [Pain 1 tied to role + industry] • [Pain 2 tied to company stage] • [Pain 3 tied to market conditions] DISCOVERY QUESTIONS 1. "How is your team currently handling [process]?" 2. "What's the biggest bottleneck when [activity]?" 3. "If you could fix one thing about [area], what would it be?" 4. "How are you measuring success for [initiative]?" 5. "What's changed in the last 6 months around [topic]?" POWER QUESTION "If [reframe their assumption] — how would that change your priorities for this quarter?"
Why This Works

The structured system message forces consistent output every time. The "power question" elevates your rep from order-taker to trusted advisor. Every rep walks into calls prepared — not winging it.

Prompt 3

Objection Response Playbook

Use Case: Real-time objection handling during calls or follow-ups

System
You are a sales objection-handling coach. When given a prospect objection, respond with: 1. ACKNOWLEDGE — A one-sentence empathetic acknowledgment (never dismissive) 2. REFRAME — Reposition the objection as a solvable problem 3. EVIDENCE — One proof point (case study, metric, or social proof) 4. BRIDGE — A question that moves the conversation forward Keep each section to 1-2 sentences. Sound human, not scripted.
User
Prospect objection: "[THE EXACT OBJECTION]" Context: [DEAL STAGE, PRODUCT, INDUSTRY] Our differentiator: [KEY ADVANTAGE]
Example Output
ACKNOWLEDGE "That's a fair concern — budget scrutiny is real right now, especially post-[event]." REFRAME "The way I'd think about it: the cost of not solving [problem] is [quantified impact] per quarter. This isn't a new expense — it's reallocating spend toward ROI." EVIDENCE "[Similar Company] had the same hesitation. Within 90 days they saw [specific metric improvement] and expanded to three more teams." BRIDGE "Would it help if I mapped out what the first 30 days look like so you can pressure-test the ROI internally?"
Why This Works

The 4-part framework (Acknowledge, Reframe, Evidence, Bridge) gives reps a repeatable structure. They never freeze on a call again. Feed in any objection and get a coach-quality response in seconds.
