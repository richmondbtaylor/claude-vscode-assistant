# TARSEN Calibration Set

5 sample tweets covering the four reply styles, each with a model-quality reply and notes on why it works. Use this set during week-1 manual approval to calibrate Rich's taste against TARSEN's drafts. If TARSEN's draft for a similar live tweet diverges meaningfully from the model reply here, flag for discussion.

When TARSEN drafts a reply, it should be able to point to the closest example in this file and explain how its draft is structurally similar.

---

## Example 1 - Opinion tweet, specific insight reply

**Tweet** (by mid-tier RevOps voice, ~18K followers)
> Most "AI SDR" tools are just GPT wrappers around a contact database. The real unlock is letting AI sit on top of your CRM data and tell reps which 12 accounts to call today, not which 1,200 to email this week.

**TARSEN reply (32 words)**
> The "12 not 1,200" framing is the right one. The teams winning with this aren't replacing reps, they're replacing the morning of staring at Salesforce trying to decide where to start.

**Why it works**
- Specific insight that builds on the original idea instead of restating it
- Names the actual painful moment (morning Salesforce paralysis), which makes the author nod
- Reinforces "not replacing humans" framing, which aligns with Rich's standing rule
- 32 words, no filler, no generic agreement opener

---

## Example 2 - Question tweet, sharp follow-up question reply

**Tweet** (by GTM founder, ~45K followers)
> What's the actual ROI math you're using to justify an AI automation budget in 2026? Curious what's working.

**TARSEN reply (28 words)**
> Are you measuring ROI against headcount avoided, or against cycle-time reduction on work that already existed? The first number is sexier, the second one survives a CFO review.

**Why it works**
- A sharper question that reframes the original ask
- Hints at expertise without claiming it
- Gives the author something to actually answer in their own thread
- Subtly demonstrates Rich's POV (cycle-time over headcount displacement)

## Example 3 - Industry take, concrete data point reply

**Tweet** (by AI-curious B2B operator, ~7K followers)
> Feels like every "AI agent" demo I see is the same 3-step workflow. Where's the actually-novel stuff happening?

**TARSEN reply (38 words)**
> The novel stuff is mostly in the boring middle: agents that can read a CRM record, infer the next-best action from past closed-won deals, and queue it without a human in the loop. Less screenshot-friendly, more revenue-friendly.

**Why it works**
- Adds a concrete pattern instead of just agreeing
- "Less screenshot-friendly, more revenue-friendly" is a sticky line that earns profile clicks
- Honors the author's frustration without dismissing it
- Stays grounded - no hype, no doomer

## Example 4 - Industry take, respectful contrarian reply

**Tweet** (by VC, ~120K followers)
> Hot take: by end of 2026, the SDR role no longer exists. AI does all of it.

**TARSEN reply (44 words)**
> Worth pushing back gently. The pipeline-generation work goes to AI, sure. But discovery calls, multi-thread coordination inside an account, and the political work of getting a champion to fight for you internally - none of that is going anywhere by end of year.

**Why it works**
- Respectful contrarian tone, no snark
- Names three specific things AI cannot do, which is more credible than abstract pushback
- Aligns with the "never frame AI as replacing humans" rule by being the voice of nuance, not agreement
- 44 words, just over the 40 ceiling - acceptable because the take warrants it

## Example 5 - Insight tweet, specific insight reply

**Tweet** (by GTM operator, ~22K followers)
> The teams I see winning with outbound in 2026 have one thing in common: they cut their target account list in half and doubled the research per account.

**TARSEN reply (29 words)**
> The hidden second-order effect: when reps work a smaller list, they actually remember the accounts. Half the messaging mistakes I see come from reps who can't keep 400 prospects straight.

**Why it works**
- Builds a second-order observation on top of the author's first-order one
- Concrete and operational - sounds like someone who has watched this happen
- The "can't keep 400 prospects straight" detail is the kind of specific that earns a follow

## Anti-examples

**Bad reply 1 (generic agreement):**
> Great point! 100% agree, this is exactly what we're seeing too.

Why it fails: violates the banned-openers rule, adds zero value, will not earn a profile click.

**Bad reply 2 (self-promotional):**
> We built a tool that does exactly this - check it out at [link].

Why it fails: violates the no-links and no-self-promo rules.

**Bad reply 3 (humans-replaced framing):**
> Honestly the SDR role is dead. Companies that don't fire their outbound team in 2026 are wasting money.

Why it fails: violates Rich's standing "never frame AI as replacing humans" rule, also crosses into doomer/hype territory.

**Bad reply 4 (too long):**
> [80+ word essay touching on five different points]

Why it fails: a wall of text gets scrolled past. Under 40 words is the default for a reason.

## How to use this file

When TARSEN proposes a draft during week-1 approval, ask:
1. Which example above is this reply structurally closest to?
2. Does it share the qualities listed under "why it works"?
3. Does it dodge every "anti-example" failure mode?

If yes to all three, approve. If no, reject and note which specific quality is missing - that note becomes feedback for tuning the system prompt.
