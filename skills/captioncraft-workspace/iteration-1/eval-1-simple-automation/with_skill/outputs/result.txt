The automations saving my clients 10–15 hours a week aren't the fancy ones.

They're embarrassingly simple.

Last week I built a workflow for a sales team. Four nodes. It watches a shared inbox, pulls contact info from new lead emails, logs it to a Google Sheet, and fires a Slack message to the right rep.

That's it.

Took me 25 minutes to build.

Their team lead told me it's saving each junior SDR about 3 hours of manual data entry. Per day.

After 8 months of building automation workflows for clients, here's what I keep seeing:

— The 40-node "intelligent" systems get demoed and forgotten
— The 4-node "boring" systems get used every single day

The mistake most people make is waiting until they have a big, worthy problem to automate. But the best first automation isn't ambitious. It's just the thing you do every week that you hate doing.

Start there. Don't try to boil the ocean.

Just drain the puddle in front of you.
