Growth, RevOps, GTM Engineer, Sales Ops, WTF guys?


Everyone uses these titles. Nobody agrees on what they mean.

Here's the simplest way to think about it:

→ Growth decides what to do and where to grow.
→ RevOps owns the entire revenue journey, from product to cash.
→ Sales Ops keeps the sales team running smoothly.
→ GTM Engineer builds the technical workflows that make it all work.


Now let's break each one down 

Sales Ops → Makes the sales team run like clockwork. CRM, forecasting, territories, comp plans. If a rep has friction before booking a meeting, Sales Ops removes it.

RevOps → Covers the entire revenue journey. From product catalog to quote to cash collection. It aligns Marketing, Sales, Customer Success, and Finance under one umbrella. Where Sales Ops optimizes one team, RevOps connects every team that touches revenue. The goal: one data model, one process, zero silos.

Growth → Finds the fastest path to revenue. SEO, paid, content, product loops, conversion — whatever moves the needle. The mindset is: test fast, double down on what works, kill what doesn't.

GTM Engineer → The newest role, and the most technical. Builds the workflows that make everyone else 10x faster. Enrichment waterfalls, Clay tables, API integrations, AI sequences. If Growth decides what to do, the GTM Engineer builds how it actually runs.


At early-stage: one person wears all four hats.
At scale: they're four teams that need to talk to each other every single day.


The best setups I've seen? A clear owner for each layer.
The worst? Everyone's doing everything and nothing actually ships.

Are you agree? What’s your definition?
