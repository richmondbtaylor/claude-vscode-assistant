## SLIDE 2 — MISTAKE #1: Treating AI like a search engine

Most people type a vague question and expect a perfect answer. AI isn't Google. It's a thinking partner. The more context you give it, the better the output.

**Fix:** Instead of "Write me a bio" try "Write a 3-sentence LinkedIn bio for a business coach who helps solopreneurs go from $5K to $20K/month. Tone: confident but approachable."

---

## SLIDE 3 — MISTAKE #2: Giving up after one bad result

You tried it once. It gave you something generic. You decided AI "isn't for you." That's like quitting the gym after one workout.

**Fix:** After a weak output, say: "That's too generic. Make it more direct and add a specific example." One follow-up prompt can turn a C into an A.

---

## SLIDE 4 — MISTAKE #3: Using AI for everything at once

You got excited. You tried emails, social posts, customer service, strategy, invoices -- all in week one. Then burned out and stopped completely.

**Fix:** Pick ONE use case. Master it. Then expand. Start with whatever task drains you most each week.

---

## SLIDE 5 — MISTAKE #4: Posting AI content without editing it

AI writes fast. But unedited AI content sounds like... AI. Your audience can feel the difference between a post that sounds like you and one that sounds like everyone else.

**Fix:** Use AI to draft. Use YOUR voice to finish. Read it out loud. If you wouldn't say it, change it.

---

## SLIDE 6 — MISTAKE #5: Not saving the prompts that work

You finally got an AI output that was genuinely useful. And then you forgot exactly how you asked for it. Most people start from scratch every time. That's wasted leverage.

**Fix:** When a prompt works, save it. Build a personal "prompt library" -- even just a Google Doc. The best AI users aren't smarter. They're more organized.
