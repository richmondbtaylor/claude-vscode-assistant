# Objection Handling Scripts

Use these rebuttals during the live call. The goal is not to "overcome" the objection but to stay curious, acknowledge the concern, and see if there's a genuine fit worth exploring. Never be pushy — one rebuttal per objection is enough. If they push back twice, offer email and move on gracefully.

---

## "Not interested"

> "I understand — can I ask what you were looking for when you visited our site? Even if it's not a fit now, it helps me understand what matters to you."

*If they engage:* Listen, then offer to send relevant resources or book a short call if a fit becomes apparent.
*If they don't engage:* "No problem at all — I'll let you go. Have a great day."

---

## "We already have a vendor"

> "That's great — are you completely satisfied or open to seeing if there's a better fit? Most of our customers came from somewhere else."

*If open:* "What's the one thing you wish your current solution did better?" Then pivot to how your product addresses it.
*If fully satisfied:* "Totally fair. I'll keep you on our radar in case that changes. Thanks for your time."

---

## "Just researching"

> "Perfect timing then — a quick call now could actually save you hours of research. I can tell you exactly how we compare to the main alternatives in 10 minutes. Does [Time Slot 1] or [Time Slot 2] work?"

*Works best for people in early evaluation who haven't committed to a direction.*

---

## "How did you find me?" / "How did you get my number?"

> "We noticed your team exploring our site and used standard business contact databases to reach the right person — it's how we make sure we're talking to the right person rather than just calling a general line. Happy to explain more if you're curious."

*This is the most important objection to handle calmly and transparently. Do not be defensive or evasive. If they seem uncomfortable:*

> "I completely understand. If you'd prefer we reach out via email going forward, I can do that instead. What's the best address?"

---

## "Send me info via email"

> "I can definitely do that — but a 15-minute call will honestly be way more valuable than an email you might not get to. Does [Time Slot 1] or [Time Slot 2] work, or is there a better time this week?"

*If they insist on email:*
> "Absolutely — I'll send something over in the next few minutes. What topics are most relevant to you right now?"

Send the follow-up email within 5 minutes. Subject: "Quick follow-up from [Your Name] at [Company]". Keep it under 5 sentences with a single clear CTA to book a call.

---

## "We don't have budget right now"

> "That makes sense — I'm not trying to sell you something today. I just want to make sure you know what's possible so that when the timing is right, you're not starting from scratch. Is a 15-minute intro call something you'd be open to this month?"

---

## "I'm not the decision maker"

> "I appreciate you being upfront about that. Who would be the right person for me to connect with? And would it be okay if I mentioned your name when I reach out?"

*Log the referred contact. If they provide a name, add to the enrichment queue with a note that the referral came from this conversation.*

---

## Call drop mid-conversation

Wait exactly 2 minutes, then call back once.

Opening: "Hi [Name], sorry we got disconnected — just wanted to make sure we finished the conversation. Is now still okay?"

If no answer on callback, leave a voicemail and send a brief email.
