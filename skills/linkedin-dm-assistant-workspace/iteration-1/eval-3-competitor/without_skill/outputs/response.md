# Eval 3 Baseline — Competitor (Derek Solis)

## Situation Analysis

**Who is Derek:** Founder of an AI automation agency focused on e-commerce brands (Shopify, Klaviyo, Make.com, Zapier). Team of 8, 6 years in the space. He reached out with a "collaboration or referral" framing.

**The tension:** Derek is a direct competitor. Both he and Bishop AI build custom automations for clients. The overlap is significant — the pitch for "collaboration or referral" is vague and could be a precursor to a sales pitch, a partnership probe, or genuine networking.

**Rich's constraint:** Does not want to pitch competitors. Should also avoid being pitched, getting drawn into a time-wasting back-and-forth, or appearing rude to someone in his professional network.

**The right tone:** Warm but non-committal. Acknowledge the common ground, be honest about the overlap, leave the door open without inviting a sales call.

---

## Recommended Response

Hey Derek — thanks for reaching out! Always good to connect with others in the automation space.

Sounds like you've built something solid — Shopify and Klaviyo workflows are no joke. Our work at Bishop AI overlaps a fair amount (custom AI agents and automations), so we're probably more parallel than complementary, but I'm happy to be a connection in the space.

If something specific ever comes up where it makes sense to loop each other in, I'm open to it. Best of luck with the agency!

— Rich

---

## Reasoning

- **"Parallel than complementary"** is a polite, honest signal that Rich sees them as competitors without being blunt or awkward about it.
- Acknowledging his work (Shopify + Klaviyo) shows Rich actually read his profile, which keeps it human.
- **No call-to-action, no meeting invite** — this avoids opening the door to a sales pitch or a time-consuming exploratory call with no upside.
- Leaves the relationship positive and professional — the space is small, burning bridges with a peer is unnecessary.
- Short and direct — respects both parties' time.
